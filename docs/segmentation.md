# Segmentation accuracy and latency

Reproduce everything on this page with, in about ten seconds and with no ROS:

```bash
make segmentation
```

## The dataset, and why it is synthetic

The brief allows SemanticKITTI. It is not used, for two reasons.

SemanticKITTI is an outdoor automotive dataset under a **non-commercial research
licence**, and its scene statistics — road surface, parked cars, vegetation —
have almost nothing to do with the geometry this pipeline has to survive. What
actually breaks warehouse segmentation is shelf tops masquerading as floor, 10 cm
racking uprights at 15 m, and people standing against a rack face.

`amr_perception.core.synthetic` ray-traces exactly those cases against the same
geometry constants that generate the Gazebo world, comes with per-point semantic
and instance ground truth for free, and carries no licence encumbrance at all.

The sensor model is a spinning 16-ring unit, 1800 azimuth steps at 0.2°, with
1 cm range noise and 1% dropout — the same values configured on the Gazebo ray
sensor. That matters more than it sounds: the property that dominates warehouse
clustering is that **vertical spacing between rings grows linearly with range**,
so distant objects fragment. Uniform random sampling in a box, which is what most
synthetic point-cloud tests use, does not have that property and will make a
clustering tolerance look fine when it is not.

**Dataset licence:** none required. The scene is generated by code in this
repository, MIT licensed.

## Results

25 scans taken along the aisle centreline from x = −12 m to +12 m, so that range
— the dominant driver of difficulty — is properly sampled rather than
characterised at one spot.

### Ground removal (per point)

| | |
|---|---|
| Precision | 0.9608 |
| Recall | **1.0000** |
| F1 | 0.9800 |
| IoU | 0.9608 |
| False negatives | **0** object points discarded as ground |
| False positives | 6417 ground points kept as obstacles |

Recall is the safety-relevant direction and it is total: no object point is ever
discarded as floor. The test suite asserts something stronger — that no labelled
object is *entirely* absorbed into the ground plane, since individual points
being lost is acceptable but an object vanishing cannot be recovered downstream.

The 4% precision shortfall is not a defect and would not be worth "fixing". Those
points are the bottom few centimetres of walls, racking uprights and pallets,
which lie inside the 0.06 m inlier band and are **geometrically indistinguishable
from floor at that threshold**. The test suite pins this down: every false
positive must lie within 0.061 m of the fitted plane. A false positive well above
the floor would mean part of a real obstacle had been discarded, and that would
be a defect.

### Clustering (per object)

Ground truth is `Scene.object_groups()` — scene primitives grouped into connected
components under the clustering radius — not raw primitives.

That distinction is the difference between a meaningful metric and a worthless
one. The scene models a racking bay as separate uprights, beams, pallets and
cartons, but they are all in physical contact. No connectivity-based clustering
can separate them and none should be expected to. Scored against raw primitives
this pipeline reports **21% recovery**; scored against connected objects, which
is the question a Euclidean clusterer actually answers, it reports 74%. The first
number measures how finely the scene was modelled and nothing else.

| | |
|---|---|
| Objects present | 163 |
| Recovered | **121 (74.2%)** |
| Under-segmented (merged into a larger cluster) | 15 |
| Over-segmented (split across clusters) | 23 |
| Spurious (matching no labelled object) | **0** |

By class:

| Class | Recovered | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| **dynamic** (people) | 30 / 32 | **93.8%** |
| pallet | 33 / 43 | 76.7% |
| structure | 58 / 88 | 65.9% |

Zero spurious clusters matters: a false detection stops the robot for no reason,
and in a narrow aisle a stopped robot blocks the aisle.

The class ordering is the right way round. People — the thing that must not be
missed — are recovered at 93.8%. Structure scores lowest because long runs of
racking fragment at range, which is over-segmentation: the object is still
detected and still avoided, it is just reported as several clusters. That is a
far better failure than under-segmentation, where a person merged into a rack
face disappears as a distinct object.

### The range-adaptive tolerance, and what it bought

Before adaptation, people were recovered at **62.5%**. The cause was concrete: at
2° between rings the vertical spacing on a surface is `0.035 × range`, so 0.42 m
at 12 m — larger than the 0.35 m tolerance. A person 11 m away produced 41
returns that fragmented into per-ring slices of about 8 points each, every one
below `min_points`, and the person was detected as **nothing at all**.

Merging components whose bounding boxes fall within `1.3 × 0.0349 × range`,
capped at 0.80 m, took people to 93.8%. The measured cost:

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| People recovered | 62.5% | 93.8% |
| Overall recovery | 70.6% | 74.2% |
| Under-segmented | 0 | 15 |
| Clustering latency (median) | 9.1 ms | 13.9 ms |

The under-segmentation is entirely additional racking merging, which is the
trade being made deliberately. The 0.80 m cap is what stops it becoming a person
merging into a rack face — see [parameter-rationale.md](parameter-rationale.md).

### Latency

Per scan, over 25 scans of ~28 500 points each, on one core of a laptop CPU:

| Stage | Median | p95 |
|---|---|---|
| Ground removal | 14.0 ms | 15.3 ms |
| Clustering | 13.9 ms | 17.2 ms |
| Occupancy grid | 6.3 ms | 7.4 ms |
| **Total** | **34.2 ms** | **38.7 ms** |

Budget at 10 Hz is 100 ms. Maximum observed 43.3 ms; **0 of 25 scans over
budget**.

Two optimisations account for most of the headroom, and both were made because
the first implementation missed the budget at ~200 ms per scan:

* **Ground removal, 74 ms → 13.5 ms.** Hypotheses are scored against a 4000-point
  subsample and evaluated in chunks of 64 in a single matmul, rather than one at
  a time against all 28k points. A one-at-a-time loop is roughly 90% numpy call
  overhead. Chunking rather than one big batch preserves the adaptive stopping
  rule. The winning plane is re-evaluated against the full cloud, so the output
  is bit-identical — verified by `test_subsampled_scoring_matches_full_scoring`.
* **Clustering, 121 ms → 8.9 ms.** Connected components moved from a Python
  union-find loop over a half-million-edge list to `scipy.sparse.csgraph`.
  Identical output.

## Zone occupancy

Seven zones: six pick faces and a charger bay, generated alongside the world so
they cannot drift from the geometry.

A pick-face zone is the strip of *aisle* a person stands in to work the face. It
stops 0.15 m short of the rack face and does not extend into the bay at all.

That is the whole design, and getting it wrong is the most likely way to produce
a plausible-looking but useless configuration. A zone drawn across the rack face
contains its own uprights, beams and stock — all permanent static returns — so
it reads occupied forever and the signal is worthless. The first version of the
generator did exactly this; `test_pick_faces_clear_the_racking` and
`test_zones_are_clear_of_static_structure_in_a_real_scan` now assert it cannot
recur, the latter by running a full scan of the scene with all people removed and
requiring every pick face to read clear.

Occupancy is decided on **raw point count inside the prism, not cluster
centroids**. A person standing at the very edge of a zone has a centroid outside
it, and centroid-based occupancy declares that zone clear.

State is debounced with asymmetric hysteresis: 2 frames to latch occupied,
5 frames to release. At 10 Hz that is 200 ms to notice someone and 500 ms to
forget them, which covers the worst observed occlusion gap behind a racking
upright. Quick to notice a person, slow to forget them — deliberately.

The publisher does **not** report "clear" when it cannot trust itself. If the
`map → base_footprint` transform is missing or stale, or AMCL's position variance
exceeds 0.25 m², it republishes the last state with `transform_valid: false` and
lets the consumer decide. Losing the transform is not evidence that a zone became
empty, and a zone publisher that quietly reports clear when it has lost track of
where it is is the most dangerous thing this pipeline could do.
